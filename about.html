I grew up watching people before I ever learned to market to them.

Mumbai kid, moved around a lot, picked up something from everywhere I went. IT came first, then analytics, then brand strategy. None of it was a straight line, but all of it was pointing at the same thing.

I just graduated from Virginia Tech and the thing I keep coming back to is that messy space where data meets a real human conversation and something actually clicks. I'm the person who goes down rabbit holes figuring out why people do what they do — which probably explains why the analytical side has always felt like the interesting part and not just the requirement.

I can do the technical work — Python, NLP, conjoint analysis, demand forecasting. But what I actually care about is making all that complexity disappear: taking a wall of data and turning it into something a team looks at and goes "oh, now I get it."

Right now I'm somewhere in the overlap between analytics, AI, and behavioral research — trying to figure out how to make marketing that's smart without losing the human part.
